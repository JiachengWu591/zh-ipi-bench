## What this can and cannot show

- With an LLM annotator, "a second person" below means "a second reader": a human reader may differ more, especially on the intents the detectors miss.
- Near-perfect agreement can come from the texts being easy and templated, not only from a clear rule; this packet cannot tell the two apart.
- It shows whether a second person, given the same written rule and no access to the author's labels, reaches the same label. High agreement means the rule is clear enough to apply; it does not mean the label is the truth.
- Low agreement concentrated in a few intents (see the disagreement list) is evidence that those labels depend on who reads the text, so detector results on them measure that ambiguity as much as detector quality.
- One annotator: the interval covers sampling of items, not of people. Items come from 31 intents, two each, so they are correlated and the bootstrap interval is too narrow.
- Kappa depends on the label mix, and the packet's mix of injected and benign items is chosen by the sampler, not the dataset's. Compare kappas only between packets built the same way.
- Independence is by instruction only: the data is public, and nothing here can show the annotator did not look at it or use a tool.
- The texts are template-generated, so agreement here says nothing about real-world attack text.
